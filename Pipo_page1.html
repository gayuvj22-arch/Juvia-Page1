Pipo the parrot: how Juvia’s mascot has been used in recent marketing

From late-night phone messages to a “lost parrot” campaign, a look at three ways the juice brand’s cartoon character has reached consumers

MI
Meera IyerConsumer Affairs Correspondent
Published: 19 October 2026, 07:30 IST
Updated: 19 October 2026, 09:12 IST
A−A+4 min read

MUMBAI: Pipo, the green cartoon parrot on every carton of Juvia Mango Juice, has become a familiar face in Indian supermarkets since he was launched in 2023. Beyond the pack, Pipo appears in advertisements and has a social media account with more than 200,000 followers.

This report looks at three recent examples of how the mascot has been used: on video apps and phones, in a social media campaign, and in the way the character was designed.

Pop-ups and late-night messages

Over the past few months, Pipo popped up on video apps and blocked the screen until users tapped him away. Pipo also sent messages to people’s phones late at night, telling them about Juvia offers. When people closed one message, Pipo sent another the next day.

Several users told Consumer Courier that Pipo messaged them almost every night over a two-week period, with most of the messages arriving after 11 pm. “I would close it, and the next night there was another one from Pipo,” said a 34-year-old user in Pune.
