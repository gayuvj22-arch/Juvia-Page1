Pipo the parrot: how Juvia’s mascot has been used in recent marketing

From late-night phone messages to a “lost parrot” campaign, a look at three ways the juice brand’s cartoon character has reached consumers

How Pipo was designed

Internal documents from Juvia, seen by Consumer Courier, show how the company created Pipo. Juvia designed him to look like a soft toy that small children would trust, and named young children as his target audience.

A product, not a friend. He exists to move juice.How Juvia’s internal documents describe Pipo

The documents say the aim was to get children to keep asking their parents for Juvia. Juvia did not tell parents any of this.

The brief, which is marked as internal and confidential, also sets out how Juvia planned Pipo’s look and personality, and how he should be used on packs, in advertising and on social media.
